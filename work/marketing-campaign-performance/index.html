Marketing Campaign Performance

What actually drove conversion across Facebook and Google Ads campaigns — tested with logistic regression, not guessed.

Context

Campaign spend was split across Facebook and Google Ads, and performance was being judged on headline totals — which platform spent more, which reported more clicks.

The question

Which customer actions and campaign attributes are actually associated with conversion, once the obvious volume differences between platforms are accounted for?

The data

Campaign-level performance data from both platforms, covering impressions, clicks, downstream customer actions and conversion outcomes. Metric definitions do not line up perfectly between the two platforms, so the first job was agreeing a comparable grain before any modelling.

Approach

Exploratory analysis first: distributions, trends over the campaign period, and the relationships between customer actions and conversion. Correlation alone was not treated as an answer.

A logistic regression model was then fitted on conversion, so the effect of individual factors could be read with the others held steady, rather than inferred from a ranked table of averages.

  • Exploratory data analysis and trend inspection
  • Relationship mapping between customer actions
  • Logistic regression on conversion outcome

What changed

The reporting moved from describing what each platform spent to stating which behaviours preceded conversion — a difference that changes where the next budget goes.
